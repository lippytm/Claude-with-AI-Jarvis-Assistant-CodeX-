**Documentation clarification only. No new license, restriction, ownership assignment,
or effective date is adopted here. Owner approval and legal review remain pending.**

## Existing files

The top-level [LICENSE](LICENSE) contains the full CC0 1.0 Universal text.
It remains unchanged. The three files below are preserved as historical draft proposals:

| File | Proposal recorded | Review status |
|---|---|---|
| [LICENSE-CODE.md](LICENSE-CODE.md) | Proprietary and shortened “MIT-style” alternatives | Neither alternative selected; not the complete MIT license |
| [LICENSE-CONTENT.md](LICENSE-CONTENT.md) | A summary patterned on CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 | Placeholder; no finalized content license or path inventory |
| [LICENSE-COMMERCIAL.md](LICENSE-COMMERCIAL.md) | Commercial approval requirements and brand claims | Placeholder; no finalized product agreement or verified trademark ownership |

The earlier wording that materials “are split” into three licenses described an
intended structure too definitively. That structure is a proposal, not a completed
replacement for LICENSE. Filenames and category labels alone do not resolve scope.

## Conflicts requiring review

- The code draft both denies permission and offers permission without choosing a branch.
  Its “recommended default” is an earlier proposal, not owner approval.
- The content draft proposes attribution, noncommercial, and no-derivatives conditions.
  It cannot be treated as adding those conditions to material already validly released
  under CC0. Its summary also differs from the actual BY-NC-ND license, which allows
  producing and reproducing noncommercial adaptations but not sharing them.
- The commercial draft's approval requirement for any product built from this repository
  conflicts with the permissions available where CC0 applies. Future MIT or Apache-2.0
  software would also permit commercial use subject to their conditions.
- The drafts name an intended LLC and describe formation as pending. This does not
  establish present ownership, completed formation, IP transfer, or trademark rights.

The applicable scope for particular historical files requires review of release history,
not a new blanket statement that all assets were or were not dedicated under CC0.

## Existing releases and future choices

CC0 is designed as an irrevocable waiver with a fallback license where necessary.
A future license change does not withdraw permissions already validly provided for the
covered material. CC0 leaves patent and trademark rights unaffected and does not clear
other people's rights. See the [official CC0 terms](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/legalcode.en).

New copyrightable contributions may have different terms if the necessary rights are
held and those terms are clearly established before release. A new version number or
a new LICENSE file does not restore exclusivity over reused legacy material.
See [Copyright Office Circular 14](https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ14.pdf).

## Proposed future organization — not adopted

Separate reusable software, educational prose, original creative assets, and paid
service/product agreements. Commercial offerings may include openly licensed software;
selling something does not itself require proprietary licensing.

The [owner review brief](docs/licensing-owner-review.md) compares CC0, MIT, Apache-2.0,
and proprietary options and describes a possible structure. Its category names and
example directories are planning suggestions only; they do not change current grants.

Before implementing any selection, identify the exact paths, versions, owners,
third-party obligations, and legacy material. Obtain explicit owner approval of the
specific texts and scope, with counsel reviewing historical CC0 coverage, entity/IP
assignments, creative-content rights, AI/personality rights, and commercial terms.
Do not replace LICENSE or finalize the three draft files as part of this clarification.
